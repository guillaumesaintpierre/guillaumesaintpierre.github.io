Transient Laser Heating of a Steel Plate

A finite-volume study of heating and shielding-gas convection in AISI 316L.

MY ROLE

Group project; MATLAB code transcribed from the team submission

The aim was to understand how a strong localized heat source and nonuniform convective cooling reshape the transient temperature field in a stainless-steel plate, and whether a compact numerical model reproduces selected analytical results.

ENGINEERING NOTE 01

01 — Physical model and geometry

We treated a 316L stainless-steel plate exposed to a prescribed laser heat input. The temperature evolves through internal conduction and boundary losses; the shielding gas introduces a spatially varying convective coefficient concentrated around the gas-jet region.

The team selected a compact 5 × 5 finite-volume mesh and an explicit time integration with Δt = 2 s. A finite-volume energy balance is written for each control volume, with conduction to adjacent cells, laser heat input and surface convection terms. The model makes the physical balance inspectable, while the coarse grid remains a limitation for resolving steep gradients.

ENGINEERING NOTE 02

02 — Numerical implementation and evidence

For each time step the code updates local temperatures from the net conductive and boundary heat fluxes. The high-temperature zone broadens with time, while gas cooling lowers the centreline temperature. The numerical verification script independently reproduces selected values from the written MATLAB model.

The calculated 50 s numerical result is about 2.7% below the analytical comparison. This is a check on one baseline prediction, not proof of experimental accuracy or full numerical convergence.

ENGINEERING NOTE 03

03 — Interpretation and limitations

The shielding gas acts as a localized energy sink, making the temperature distribution spatially nonuniform even under otherwise similar laser heating. The study demonstrates why the boundary-condition model matters as much as the internal heat-conduction calculation.

Limitations include an intentionally coarse mesh, simplified material properties and boundary conditions, and no temperature-dependent heat-source validation. Refining the mesh, testing the explicit stability limit and measuring actual surface temperatures would be the natural extensions.
