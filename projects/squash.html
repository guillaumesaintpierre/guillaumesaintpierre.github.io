ENGINEERING NOTE 01

01 — Functional requirements and concept selection

The target system stores energy from a manual crank, compresses a spring, meters squash balls into the launch path and releases a guided launching member. The specification included a five-ball magazine, one ball every five seconds, adjustable elevation angles and a nominal 90 km/h sizing target within an 80–130 km/h launch-speed requirement.

Our concept selection weighed wheel-driven, impact-driven and spring-based mechanisms. We chose a hand-cranked two-stage gearbox, a spring mechanism and synchronized gravity feed, trading off manual effort, timing, mechanical complexity and repeatability.

ENGINEERING NOTE 02

02 — Analytical sizing and mechanical design

The submitted calculations used 20 N manual force on a 125 mm crank radius, giving 2.5 N·m input torque and 50 N·m after the ideal 20:1 reduction. A selected spring stiffness of 5,980 N/m and 115 mm compression imply a spring force of approximately 688 N at maximum compression.

Espring = ½ k x² ≈ 39.54 J from specified k and stroke

There is a useful engineering caveat: the original report gives about 40.9 J at one point, while the stated parameters imply 39.54 J and the nominal kinetic energy requirement is around 40.31 J before losses. The energy margin therefore needs reconciliation and physical validation. I would not claim the device demonstrated a 90 km/h launch.

The project also considered shaft and key strength, gear and bearing losses, contact pressure, critical fits, safety around a loaded spring and anti-reverse pawl geometry.

ENGINEERING NOTE 03

03 — CAD, assembly and manufacturing

The design package includes detailed CATIA models, a 33-page set of 2D manufacturing/assembly drawings, material selection, tolerances, part numbering and an assembly sequence. Turning and milling operations were chosen to match the forms of shafts, plates, brackets and guides. The report discusses tolerance callouts such as ISO 2768 and interfaces for gears, bearings and shafts.

The submitted CATIA model indicates a mass around 10.49 kg, below the specified 15 kg design limit. It remains a model estimate rather than a measured prototype. This was a four-person EPFL project and the documented work was shared across design stages.
