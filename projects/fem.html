ENGINEERING NOTE 01

01 — Structural analysis foundations

In the first assignment we implemented linear truss equilibrium using element stiffness matrices, global assembly, support constraints and reaction-force recovery. Analytical equilibrium checks ensured that FEM support reactions were mechanically consistent. My submitted subassignments also compared material-nonlinear solution strategies: pure Euler, corrected Euler, Newton–Raphson and modified Newton–Raphson.

At the reference load, full Newton–Raphson converged with 61 iterations / 61 matrix factorizations, while modified Newton–Raphson required 183 iterations but only 18 factorizations. This isolates a practical computational trade-off: updating the tangent stiffness improves convergence per iteration, while reusing a factorization can lower cost per correction.

ENGINEERING NOTE 02

02 — Solver performance, stress and convergence

The second assignment studied matrix bandwidth, node numbering and storage. In a structured Q4 beam example with 14,350 degrees of freedom, theoretical full storage was about 1.65 GB, versus about 8.5 MB for the narrow band representation with the original numbering. We benchmarked assembly and solve time in Fortran.

Other team subassignments extended the code toward stress recovery around fillets, mesh-convergence studies and modal analysis of a coupled L-shaped structure. The report estimated an extrapolated fillet stress around 225 MPa and tip deflection around 3.12 mm for its specified load and material. Those tasks were led by Lukas and are included as team results, not claimed as my independent work.

ENGINEERING NOTE 03

03 — Final project: thermal finite elements

The final group project focuses on heat conduction and sequential thermoelastic coupling. The current Fortran code computes an element conductivity matrix for a four-node isoparametric quadrilateral using 2 × 2 Gauss quadrature, assembles the global thermal matrix and applies thermal boundary temperatures, fluxes and volume sources.

A separate element-level test checks conductivity-matrix symmetry, preservation of a constant temperature field, a body source and a surface flux. A manufactured-solution study refines the mesh from 4 × 4 to 32 × 32 elements and measures nodal errors against known analytical fields.

ENGINEERING NOTE 04

04 — The planned thermoelastic coupling

The intended sequential workflow first solves the temperature field, then constructs equivalent nodal loads from thermal expansion, and finally solves the constrained mechanical equilibrium. The constitutive law links thermal strain and elastic stress; constraints turn otherwise free expansion into internal stress.

σ = C ( ε − α ΔT ) Thermoelastic constitutive relation · planned mechanical extension

The final verification plan includes free thermal expansion, restrained expansion, analytical thermal/structural benchmarks, a mesh study and an application with more realistic geometry. As of 19 November 2026, the mechanical coupling and final submission were not present in the verified repository. The completed report will be added after the December group assignment.
